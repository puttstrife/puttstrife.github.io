Services Website

I designed a service website around a practical decision: help visitors understand the offer, work out whether it fits, and book a well-prepared conversation.

Product design & development·Service design · Booking flow

The Situation

Before someone books, they need to know what they are booking.

An AI-enabled service can sound promising while still leaving the important questions unanswered: What is included? Is it relevant to my work? What happens when I ask for a demo?

The site needed to replace an open-ended sales conversation with a clearer path from first impression to a useful, informed request.

The Approach

Turn interest into a considered next step.

I organized the experience around explanation, comparison, and preparation. Public pages introduce the service and pricing. A calculator gives visitors a way to explore scope. The booking flow asks for the business context and bottleneck that make a follow-up conversation more useful.

Behind the form, the service checks that key fields, dates, and time slots are valid before handing the request to the configured operations tools.

Key Moments

Make the decision easier before making the ask.

How It Works

A booking request should be intentional, not accidental.

What It Enables

Clear service story

Focused pages explain the offering before asking a visitor to get in touch.

Self-guided comparison

Pricing and calculation tools let someone explore scope before they commit to a conversation.

Prepared bookings

The request flow captures a visitor’s business type and bottleneck alongside the requested slot.

Safe handoff

Validation and configuration checks prevent the system from silently accepting an incomplete request.

Conclusion

“The best booking flow does not just fill a calendar. It helps both people arrive prepared.”

This is a service experience designed to make the next step feel specific and useful. Its calendar, email, and customer-record handoffs depend on environment configuration, so the case study describes the implemented workflow rather than claiming those external services are live.
