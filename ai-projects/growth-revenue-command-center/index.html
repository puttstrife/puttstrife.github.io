Growth & Revenue
Command Center

I designed a command center for the question teams ask every morning: where is performance moving, and where should we look first?

Product design & development·Decision support · Sample-data prototype

The Situation

A dashboard is only useful when it is honest about its view of the business.

Traffic, conversion, audience, email, and revenue signals often live in different places. That makes it hard to begin the day with one clear question: what changed, and where should the team look next?

Just as important, a summary view should not pretend to be live or complete when it is not. The design needed to make data coverage visible, not hide it behind polished charts.

The Approach

Give the team one starting point, with its limits in plain sight.

I grouped acquisition, conversion, email engagement, audience mix, and monetization into a single operating view. Range, site, and page controls change the scope of the view; headline measures and opportunity signals then recalculate from that selection.

This is explicitly a prototype using deterministic sample data. It makes no external API requests. That boundary is part of the product, not a disclaimer added at the end.

Key Moments

From a wide view to a useful next question.

How It Works

Every number is tied to the selected scope.

What It Enables

One starting view

Acquisition, conversion, engagement, audience, and monetization can be considered together.

Scoped comparison

Range, site, and page filters reshape the measures shown in the interface.

Transparent signals

Opportunity prompts are based on the measures available in the selected sample data.

Accessible detail

Charts are supported by labels, tooltips, and table equivalents.

Conclusion

“A useful command center does not promise certainty. It makes the next question easier to ask.”

This project is a prototype, not a live analytics integration. Its value is in testing how a broad operating picture can stay clear, inspectable, and honest about the data it has.
