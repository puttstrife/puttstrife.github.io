Digital Tribute
Platform

I designed a digital service that gives people a respectful way to leave a message, while giving the team a dependable workflow to carry it through.

Product design & development·Public service · Operations workflow

The Situation

A personal act needs care on both sides of the screen.

Leaving a tribute is a personal moment. People need to understand what they are being asked to do, what will happen next, and how their message will be represented.

Behind that moment is a different kind of care: a team needs to schedule work, keep a clear record of each step, and follow through reliably. The service had to make both journeys feel deliberate.

The Approach

A calm public journey, connected to a clear operational one.

I brought submission, confirmation, sharing, and scheduled fulfillment into one service model. The public side guides a visitor through a small set of visible steps. The operations side surfaces the daily queue, scheduled-run detail, proofs, reports, and follow-up tools.

Rather than treating the public site and internal workspace as separate products, the design connects them through the same status and fulfillment flow.

Key Moments

Make a sensitive service understandable.

How It Works

Care also means making completion traceable.

What It Enables

Guided submissions

A structured public flow helps visitors move from intention to confirmation.

Shared representation

Eligible entries can appear in a public wall and on individual share pages.

Scheduled delivery

The service assigns submissions to an eligible scheduled run, based on its rules and cutoff.

Operational clarity

A protected console provides queues, reports, and audit history for the people carrying the service forward.

Conclusion

“A respectful digital service should be clear to the person submitting—and dependable to the people fulfilling it.”

The project pairs an emotionally considered public experience with the practical work required to deliver it. Payment and notification connections are configuration-dependent, so the work is presented as a designed and implemented service flow—not as a claim about a live deployment.
