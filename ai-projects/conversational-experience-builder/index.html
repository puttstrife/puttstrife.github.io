Conversational
Experience Builder

I turned a one-off scripted chat into a reusable conversation builder, so a team could shape, test, and revise the experience without rebuilding it.

Product design & development·Content authoring · Guided conversations

The Situation

A good conversation should be easy to improve.

Guided chats often begin as carefully written scripts. But once every message, pause, question, and next step is buried in code, even a small change becomes a development task.

The challenge was to give a content team control over the shape and pace of a conversation without losing the feeling of a crafted, personal exchange.

The Approach

One definition. Two useful views.

I designed the experience around a shared conversation definition. In the editor, a team can arrange stages, messages, questions, choices, calls to action, visual treatment, and pacing. In the player, that same definition becomes a guided chat.

The player can reveal messages over time, collect answers, carry an answer into later copy, offer branches, and export a transcript. The result is a conversation that feels authored rather than assembled from a generic form.

Key Moments

Control the rhythm, not just the words.

How It Works

The player reads the flow instead of hard-coding it.

What It Enables

Editable journeys

Scripts can be structured as stages, beats, questions, choices, and calls to action.

Responsive storytelling

Answers can be carried into later copy, creating a more personal path through the experience.

Portable drafts

Flows can be duplicated and moved through JSON export and import.

Optional context

Approximate location can be used when available, without preventing the conversation from continuing when it is not.

Conclusion

“A conversational interface becomes more useful when the people who write it can also shape how it unfolds.”

This work turns a scripted experience into a reusable authoring tool. The current CMS keeps drafts in the browser, making it a focused prototype for shaping and testing flows rather than a shared publishing system.
